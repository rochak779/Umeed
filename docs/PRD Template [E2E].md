# **How To Use This Template**

A PRD is a document that explains a problem, and then explains the plan to fix it. PRD stands for Product Requirements Document. This template has two big parts. The first part covers the problem space. The second part covers the solution space. You should always finish the problem space before you start the solution space, because a good solution only makes sense once you truly understand the problem.

Every subsection below has the same parts. The first part tells you what to write. The second part gives you questions to help you think. The third part is a blank space for your own answer. Some subsections also have a mentor tip in an orange box, which gives extra advice.

Some subsections ask you to cite your sources. Whenever you use a number, a quote, or a fact from outside your own head, write down exactly where it came from, such as a report name, a website, or the name of the person you interviewed. A fact with no source is just a guess.

At the end of most major sections, you will find a teal box called Insight & Conclusion. This is the most important box in the whole template. Facts alone do not make a PRD strong. What makes it strong is stepping back and saying what those facts mean, and what you will do differently because of them. Do not skip these boxes, and do not just repeat your answer from above. Say the so what.

This template does not belong to one type of business. You can use it for an app, a physical product, a service, a school project, or a nonprofit idea. Wherever you see a word like product or user, replace it in your head with whatever fits your own case study.
